# SYNTHETIC: Prohibited documentation shortcuts

SYNTHETIC TRAINING DOCUMENT. Project-authored on 2026-10-03, version 1.
The fictional Kestrel training service and its policies are not real providers.
No real patient information appears in this document.

## Why this policy exists

A documentation exercise can produce a fluent but inaccurate record when an
author fills gaps with customary wording. The fictional service prohibits that
shortcut. A draft must remain distinguishable from an approved record, and
unknown information must remain distinguishable from a negative finding.

## Actions that are not permitted

Do not copy a previous examination into a new encounter as though it were
performed again. Do not write that a symptom was denied when no denial was
supplied. Do not convert an unanswered screening item into a negative result.
Do not record counselling, consent, teach-back or a completed referral merely
because the template contains a space for that activity.

Do not expand an ambiguous abbreviation into a diagnosis without an identified
source. Do not insert a medicine dose from memory or from a different person's
example. Do not interpret the absence of a listed interaction as evidence that
two therapies are compatible. A missing contraindication statement does not
establish that a treatment is safe.

## Copying and corrections

Copied material must retain its origin and historical status. If a source has
been superseded, explain the version relationship when it is known; do not
invent that relationship from the order in which files were uploaded. A
correction retains the original assertion in the training audit history and
identifies the supporting replacement source.

## Review questions

The reviewer checks whether every asserted action actually appears in the
supplied exercise material. Review also checks negation, uncertainty, chronology
and the distinction between an instruction and an event that has occurred.
Unresolved facts return to the responsible author as open documentation items.

## Limits

This is negative evidence about a fictional documentation process. It is not
a prescribing resource or a substitute for an external guideline. It provides
no patient record, drug regimen, clinical exception or emergency protocol.
