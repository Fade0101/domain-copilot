# SYNTHETIC: Correction and amendment history

SYNTHETIC TRAINING DOCUMENT. Project-authored on 2026-10-03, version 1.
The fictional Kestrel training service is not a healthcare provider. This
document describes an exercise policy and contains no real patient information.

## Purpose

A corrected draft should make it possible to identify what changed and why.
The fictional amendment record preserves the original statement, the proposed
replacement, the reason for correction and the evidence supporting it. It does
not claim that a clinical event changed when only its documentation changed.

## Source and chronology

Attribute a correction to the supplied source. Distinguish the date of an
underlying event from the date of its recording when both are available. If an
exercise gives only one date, preserve its stated meaning and leave the other
unspecified. Never invent a retrospective signature or review timestamp.

## Kinds of correction

A transcription correction addresses a copying error. A clarification supplies
additional meaning from an identified source. A newly reported fact may add
information without proving that the original author knew it earlier. The
amendment record keeps these situations distinct rather than treating every
change as a replacement of clinical judgement.

## Review requirements

The reviewer checks that negation, uncertainty, attribution and version context
survive the correction. A statement should not become more certain merely
because the revised wording is shorter. Conflicting evidence remains visible
until the exercise supplies a documented resolution.

## What this policy does not establish

An amendment request does not itself establish that a treatment was delivered,
a result was received or a reviewer approved the final record. This document
does not describe a production audit system, access-control mechanism or legal
retention requirement. It supplies documentation concepts for a synthetic
training corpus, not implementation requirements for the application.
