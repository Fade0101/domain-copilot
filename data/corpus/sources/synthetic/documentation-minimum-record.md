# SYNTHETIC: Minimum documentation record

SYNTHETIC TRAINING DOCUMENT. Project-authored on 2026-10-03, version 1.
The Kestrel training service is fictional. This document contains no patient
records and is not an external clinical guideline.

## Purpose and scope

This fictional policy describes how a training note records the basis for a
statement. It supplies documentation examples, not diagnosis or treatment
instructions. A complete-looking form is not proof that an assessment occurred.
Only information actually supplied in an exercise belongs in its draft record.

## Required elements

Record the purpose of the encounter, the information source, the observations
provided, the assessment attributed to the responsible reviewer, and the next
documented action. Keep reported history separate from directly observed findings.
If an outside document supplies a fact, retain its title, version and relevant
section so a reviewer can find the same passage.

A medication entry must distinguish a reported list from a reconciled list. A
blank field means that the exercise has not supplied that information. It must
not be converted into a statement that the person takes no medicines. Similarly,
an unrecorded reaction history must not become a claim of no known allergies.

## Uncertainty and attribution

Mark unavailable information as unavailable. Describe a disagreement between
sources without choosing the most convenient statement. Preserve qualifiers such
as reported, historical, pending, possible and not assessed. A factual correction
must point to its supporting information rather than simply replacing an
unfamiliar term with a more confident one.

## Review record

The training reviewer records whether the draft reflects the supplied material,
which questions remain unresolved, and who owns the next documentation action.
Do not invent a review time, signature, approval, examination or conversation.
An example template contains field labels only; it is not a completed note.

## Boundaries

This policy contains no medication doses, drug compatibility conclusions or
patient-specific recommendations. Its completeness checklist cannot establish
that a clinical intervention is appropriate. Clinical evidence must come from
an applicable, attributed source rather than from this synthetic policy.
