# SYNTHETIC: Documenting a consent conversation

SYNTHETIC TRAINING DOCUMENT. Project-authored on 2026-10-03, version 1.
This fictional Kestrel policy contains no completed consent form or patient
information. It is not a legal opinion or a rule for a real jurisdiction.

## Record what the exercise actually supplies

A training note distinguishes a planned discussion from a discussion that was
reported as completed. It records the stated purpose, the information described
as discussed, questions that were reported, and any supplied decision or
unresolved issue. It must not invent the contents of a conversation.

## Attribution and communication

Identify the source of each account. When communication assistance is explicitly
reported, preserve that description without inventing a language, interpreter,
identifier or method. Do not state that understanding was demonstrated unless
the exercise supplies evidence for that statement.

The absence of a documented question does not show that there were no questions.
An unfilled decision field is unspecified; it is not consent, refusal or lack
of capacity. A request to review a draft does not authorize the author to mark
an intervention as accepted or performed.

## Unresolved matters

List information that remains unavailable and identify the responsible review
role when supplied. Keep disagreement visible rather than resolving it through
generic template language. If an external policy is referenced, retain its
title and version so the reviewer can establish whether it applies.

## Corrections and boundaries

An amended description cites the new supplied information and does not erase
the existence of the earlier uncertainty. This fictional document defines no
legal standard for capacity, substitute decision-making or consent validity.
It supplies no treatment recommendation and cannot establish that a real
discussion, approval or clinical action has occurred.
