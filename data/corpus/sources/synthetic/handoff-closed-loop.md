# SYNTHETIC: Closed-loop handoff documentation

SYNTHETIC TRAINING DOCUMENT. Project-authored on 2026-10-03, version 1.
This policy belongs to the fictional Kestrel training service. It describes a
documentation exercise, contains no patient data and does not direct real care.

## Handoff content

A training handoff separates the reason for transfer, the information already
confirmed, the questions still open, and the action requested of the receiving
role. Each statement identifies its source. A requested action must not be
written as though the recipient has already completed it.

List pending information with an owner and the expected means of communication.
If the exercise supplies neither an owner nor an expected time, state that those
details are unspecified. Do not create a plausible recipient, contact address,
deadline or result to make the handoff appear complete.

## Communication status

The local status terms are prepared, sent, acknowledged and completed. These
terms describe communication evidence in the exercise. A message being sent does
not establish that it was read. An acknowledgement does not establish that a
requested assessment or procedure has occurred.

To record completion, the draft needs an explicit supplied completion statement
or result and its source. Retain the distinction between the time an event was
reported and the time its documentation was entered when both are supplied.
If only one is known, do not derive the other.

## Escalation and unresolved items

An unresolved handoff remains visible as an open item. The reviewer records the
missing information and the role responsible for follow-up. This fictional
policy does not prescribe a clinical urgency category, escalation deadline or
emergency response. Those decisions require an applicable real policy and the
responsible clinical team.

## Documentation boundary

This document offers positive evidence for what a fictional handoff record
contains and negative evidence against treating transmission as completion.
It supplies no drug, dose, diagnosis, result or patient-specific recommendation.
